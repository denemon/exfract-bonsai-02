# Stage 4 — new continuous anatomy

The original attached 302×404 bonsai, selected PC reference, v23 clay views and failed local A/B views were inspected. A separate read-only reviewer also viewed the same seven saved images and the attached photo. No second writer or browser was created.

## Root failure, not a texture failure

The old main trunk is a wide S-shaped band whose front/back are too parallel. A exposes its thin edges with holes; B leaves a horizontal shelf where the old band meets replacement feet. Root tips are displayed as claws or rounded legs. The old branch junctions resemble smooth fittings and repeated horns. Neither more cuts nor more periodic grooves can supply the missing body.

## Fundamental construction change

Do not load or deform any old wood mesh or field. Build one independent closed control cage: unequal rounded cross-sections with independently authored centre, width, depth and orientation. Monotone height sections prevent the lower S bend from folding back into a thin self-overlapping ribbon. Front-back depth is a substantial fraction of frontal width and changes independently. The lower cross-sections themselves spread into three unequal broad buttresses; their perimeter ends below soil and no exposed root tip is sharpened.

Remove one rectangular patch from this cage and extrude its existing boundary into one smaller child branch. Parent and child share boundary vertices from the outset: no overlapping pipe, Boolean union, cut-and-reattach root or buried horizontal collar. The parent remains stronger than the child. The underside shoulder, upper valley and parent continuation use separate control positions. Catmull–Clark subdivision only interpolates this cage; it adds no grain, bump or ornamental ribs.

Start with the lower trunk and first fork only. Compare neutral grey with no normal map, vertex AO or texture; root volume, changing section thickness and branch saddle must read at front, ±30° and +55°. One compact lower prototype is the focus. If it passes the structural volume gate, extend its original cage upward in this stage, rather than attach a cut old trunk. This gate is not a claim of finished deadwood or premium-scene quality.
